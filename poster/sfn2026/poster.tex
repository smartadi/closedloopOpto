\sectionbar{Introduction}
\begin{textbox}
Mesoscale cortical activity is linked to perception, cognition and movement. Testing what
it causes requires shaping it, not only perturbing it.\\[0.3em]
\textbf{Can feedback hold a cortical site at a target level, and which brain states make that harder?}
\end{textbox}
\vfill
\sectionbar{Results}
\begin{resultbox}{1}{We close the loop on cortex in real time}
\prow{\pn{rig}\hfil\pn{loop}}
\vspace{0.15in}\noindent\begin{minipage}[c]{19.3cm}\pn{frame}\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[c]{\dimexpr\linewidth-20cm\relax}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Wide-field GCaMP8s imaging at 35\,Hz
  \item Output: \dff{} beside the stimulation spot
  \item Loop delay 29\,ms mean (14--47\,ms)
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
\end{resultbox}
\vfill
\begin{resultbox}{2}{The average response is near-linear; single trials vary with brain state}
\prow{\pn{imp_traces}\hfil\pn{imp_dose}\hfil\pn{imp_fit}}
\prow{\pn{imp_r2}\hfil\pn{imp_state}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Suppression scales with laser power (\Rsq{} = 0.73--0.97), and a low-order
        linear model predicts held-out trial averages (median \Rsq{} = 0.85; 4 sessions, 3 mice).
  \item Single-trial variability falls at the highest motion (0.58$\times$) and rises with absolute
        2--4\,Hz power (2.35$\times$), with or without stimulation.
\end{itemize}
\end{resultbox}
\end{minipage}\hfill
% ------------------------------------------- col 2: feedback works
\begin{minipage}[t][\colH][t]{\colB}\vspace{0pt}
\begin{resultbox}{3}{Feedback holds activity at the target and reduces trial-to-trial variability}
\prow{\pn{cl_trial}\hfil\pn{cl_rmse}}
\prow{\pn{cl_avg}\hfil\pn{cl_var}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Target --5\%\,\dff{} for 3\,s, loops interleaved (15 sessions, 4 mice).
  \item RMSE 23\% lower (ratio 0.77, \pv{\sci{5.8}{8}}), in all 15 sessions.
  \item Across-trial variance 42\% lower (ratio 0.58, \pv{\sci{6.3}{6}}), in 14 of 15 sessions.
  \item Also beats the best fixed input (13\%, \pv{\sci{6.5}{4}}): not only calibration.
\end{itemize}
\end{resultbox}
\vfill
\begin{resultbox}{4}{Moving target: feedback reduces error, preview corrects the lag}
\prow{\pn{sine_avg}}
\prow{\pn{sine_lag}\hfil\pn{sine_rmse}\hfil\pn{sine_var}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item 1\,Hz target; preview gives the controller the target 143\,ms ahead. Mouse 3b shown;
        same direction in all 3 sessions (no tests).
  \item Feedback: variance 11.8 $\to$ 5.8\,(\%\,\dff)\textsuperscript{2}, median RMSE 3.59 $\to$ 2.26.
  \item Preview: lag 71$^\circ$ $\to$ 16$^\circ$ in open loop, 46$^\circ$ $\to$ 2$^\circ$ in closed loop; it does not lower the error.
\end{itemize}
\end{resultbox}
\vfill
\begin{textbox}
{\smallsize\color{ink}
\textbf{Methods.} CaMKII-tTA;tetO-GCaMP8s mice with AAV PHP.eB DLX2.0-ChrimsonR in inhibitory
interneurons, so 638\,nm light suppresses local excitatory activity. Head-fixed, awake, no task
(rig and preparation: Matveev et al., 2024). Closed-loop \dff{} uses the controller's own
trailing 40\,s mean; impulse \dff{} divides by the session mean image. Statistics: per-session log
CL/OL ratios, one-sample \textit{t}-test across sessions, checked per mouse; state slopes fitted within
session and tested across sessions; rejection ratio from a session-level mixed model.\par}
\end{textbox}
\end{minipage}\hfill
% ------------------------------------------- col 3: the main result
\begin{minipage}[t][\colH][t]{\colC}\vspace{0pt}
\begin{resultbox}{5}{Controller performance depends on brain state}
\prow{\pn{st_trials}\hfil\pn{st_unique}}
\prow{\pn{st_quart}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Initial deviation explains transient error (unique \Rsq{} = 0.53); absolute 1--4\,Hz power explains
        a modest part of steady-state error (0.11), more than any other state.
  \item \textbf{Movement:} open-loop error rises (\pv{0.011}), closed-loop error stays flat, so feedback
        removes a larger fraction of the error (CL/OL --13\% per SD, \pv{0.0023}).
  \item \textbf{Absolute 1--4\,Hz power:} closed-loop error rises (\pv{\sci{7.1}{4}}, all 15 sessions)
        about as much as open-loop error; the fraction removed does not change significantly (\pv{0.094}).
  \item One explanation: opsin and indicator kinetics and loop delay limit the controllable frequencies.
\end{itemize}
\end{resultbox}
\vfill
\begin{resultbox}{6}{Feedback partly rejects a disturbance predicted from the other hemisphere}
\prow{\pn{d_kernel}\hfil\pn{d_agl}\hfil\pn{d_rr}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A linear model fit on laser-off frames predicts the site from the contralateral hemisphere
        (median held-out \Rsq{} = 0.76, 13 sessions).
  \item Closed loop leaves less residual energy than the disturbance: rejection ratio 0.58
        (\pv{\sci{1.3}{4}}; 11 sessions, 3 mice). It removes most of the constant offset and less of the fluctuations.
\end{itemize}
\end{resultbox}
\vfill
\sectionbar{Conclusions}
\begin{textbox}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A near-linear average response is enough to design a controller that \textbf{holds a cortical
        site at a target level}, better than open loop in every session and mouse.
  \item \textbf{Performance depends on brain state:} feedback removes more of the error during movement,
        while error rises with absolute 1--4\,Hz power.
  \item Feedback rejects part of a disturbance predicted from the other hemisphere, and preview corrects
        the lag when the target moves.
\end{itemize}
