#### CONTROL
SUMMARY: Control-theory referee pass on discussion_draft.tex and methods_remark.tex. No files were edited.

What holds up:
- I checked Logemann & Curtain (2000) Theorem 4.1 against the paper itself (Numdam full text). Conditions (i)-(iv) in the remark match the theorem: an exponentially stable well-posed system, which covers input delay; G(0) > 0; phi nondecreasing and globally Lipschitz; r/G(0) in im phi; and k in (0, K*/lambda). The conclusion phi(u(t)) -> r/G(0) is part (a).
- e(t) -> 0 is not in the theorem's main statement, which gives only e in L2. Pointwise convergence comes from part (f) together with Remark 4.2(b). The citation should name those parts.
- The sign conventions are internally consistent: s = -y, G(0) > 0, e = |y_ref| - s = y - y_ref (matches Eq. error), and u' = k e gives negative feedback. However, the remark never defines e.
- The ΔF/F baseline is frozen within each trial, so the rolling normalisation does not break the static-plant reading.
- The delay arithmetic is right: 47 + 14.3 = 61.3 ms gives 2.60 Hz, and about 23.5 + 14.3 = 37.8 ms gives 4.2 Hz.
- 'Internal model of constants only' is correct for PI within a trial.
- The Desoer & Lin abstract confirms the setting: exponentially stable plant, strictly increasing dc map, PI. The low-gain condition is not in the abstract and I did not verify it.

Main problems:
1. The 2.6-4 Hz figure counts only the delay of the electronics, so it is an upper bound on what any controller could reach. The cortical response adds its own lag: 60 degrees at 1 Hz open loop, plus a damped 2.4-4.2 Hz dip mode. The closed loop shows 36 degrees of lag at 1 Hz. Together these suggest the crossover of the loop that ran is nearer 1-2 Hz, so the statement that 1-2 Hz lies below crossover is unsupported.
2. 'Beyond crossover feedback cannot attenuate' should be 'attenuates little' (|S| > 1/2 wherever |L| < 1). The Bode integral does hold for a stable open loop with delay.
3. Brammer is generalised into a false statement: some systems with one-signed inputs are controllable. The result applies to plants with a real mode.
4. Morari's SISO existence condition needs only G(0) != 0. The sign of G(0) fixes the sign the controller gain must have; it does not decide whether a controller exists.
5. The 'result' is blended across theorems. Desoer-Lin covers PI with no saturation; Logemann-Curtain covers I-only control of a linear plant with saturation and delay. No single result covers PI, saturation, delay and sampling together.
6. The claim that Fig 2 satisfies (ii) is not established. G(0) is the integral of the impulse response over all time, but the slow mode is unresolved within the 0.5 s fit window in 3 of 4 sessions. The impulse sessions are also not the controller sessions.
7. phi and condition (iv) must include the feedforward term ū and the q = 1 V clamp on the integral term, which narrows the reachable range to [max(0, ū-q), min(u_max, ū+q)]. Any standing offset of unstimulated activity must also enter (iv). Both matter for the reachability claim.
8. The minimum-variance bound is still a floor with a one-sided actuator; it is state dependence that makes it a benchmark rather than a hard floor.
9. Desoer (1969) gives stability only.
10. 'Integral term doing this work' cannot be separated from proportional action without an ablation.
11. Four cited keys are in neither refs.bib nor new_refs.bib: desoer1969slowly, logemann1999integral, fliegner2001discrete and garone2017reference. BibTeX is supplied below, checked against OpenAlex. McGinley2015waking and valley2020separation are also missing from both files.
12. Methods L901-902 repeats 'the empirical counterpart of the sensitivity function' and Results L230 repeats 'the integral term removes that offset'. Both need the same fixes as the Discussion.
[0] (wrong) QUOTE: This explains the top of the band but not 1--2\,Hz, which lies below crossover.
   PROBLEM: 2.6-4 Hz is a ceiling set by the delay of the electronics alone (omega_c*tau <~ 1); it is not the crossover of the loop that ran. The cortical response adds lag: about 60 deg at 1 Hz in open loop (Fig 5), and a damped 2.4-4.2 Hz dip mode (Results L81, natural frequency about 3.3 Hz, which alone contributes about -90 deg there). The PI integrator adds more lag. The 36-deg closed-loop lag at 1 Hz (Fig 5) suggests |L(1 Hz)| of only about 1.5-2, i.e. a crossover nearer 1-2 Hz. The claim that 1-2 Hz lies below crossover is therefore unsupported and probably wrong. Because the next sentences offer alternative accounts for 1-2 Hz, this one needs to be conditional.
   FIX: How much of the band this covers depends on the crossover of the loop that ran, which we did not measure: if it lies near the cap, delay explains only the top of the band, but if it lies nearer 1--2\,Hz, as the 36-degree closed-loop phase lag at 1\,Hz suggests (Figure~\ref{fig:figure5}), most of the 1--4\,Hz band sits at or above crossover.
[1] (wrong) QUOTE: a system driven by inputs of one sign cannot be steered in every direction~\citep{brammer1972controllability}
   PROBLEM: Stated generally, this is false. Brammer's condition (rank condition, plus no real eigenvector v of A^T with v'Bu <= 0 for all admissible u) is met by plants whose modes are all complex, e.g. an oscillator, which a one-signed input can control. What fails is a single one-signed input acting on a plant with a real mode. Brammer's result is also about null-controllability of the state, whereas the argument in this paragraph is steady-state admissibility of a set-point. LIT_REVIEW already flags this distinction.
   FIX: this is a steady-state admissibility condition rather than controllability, although a single input of one sign also cannot steer a linear system with a real mode in every direction~\citep{brammer1972controllability}
[2] (wrong) QUOTE: With a single input, whether a stabilizing integral controller exists depends only on the sign of the steady-state gain
   PROBLEM: For a stable SISO plant, Morari's condition for an integral controller to exist is only G(0) != 0. The sign of G(0) fixes the sign the integral gain must have; it does not decide whether a controller exists. The useful statement for this paper is that one controller of fixed sign serves every session whose gain keeps that sign.
   FIX: With a single input and a stable plant, a stabilizing integral controller exists whenever the steady-state gain is nonzero, and one of fixed sign serves every plant whose gain has the matching sign

[4] (overclaim) QUOTE: places the achievable bandwidth near 2.6--4\,Hz by the usual crossover criterion~\citep{skogestad2005multivariable,astrom2000limitations}.

[6] (overclaim) QUOTE: where feedback cannot attenuate and, as the price of attenuating below crossover, amplifies
   PROBLEM: Wherever |L| < 1, |S| = 1/|1+L| > 1/2, so feedback above crossover attenuates little rather than not at all. Amplification (|S| > 1) concentrates near and just above crossover.
